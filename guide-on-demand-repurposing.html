Analytics, ROI & after the show

After the live: the event is the raw material.

The live audience is often the smaller one. How to edit, package and distribute the event so it keeps working for weeks, and what to ask the production team to record.

3 min read · Updated September 2026

An event that ends when the stream stops has wasted most of what it made. The recordings, the questions, the moments (packaged well and released on a schedule) reach more people than the live show and keep delivering for the quarter. This is the process, starting with what to record.

Record for the edit, not just the archive

Ask for: a clean programme recording at high bitrate (not the compressed stream); ISO recordings of each camera and each remote guest, so the editor can re-cut; the graphics-free version (or a separate graphics track) so lower thirds can be corrected; and the audio stems. Tell the production team before the show. It changes how they set up the recorders. The encoder guide explains the quality difference.

The on-demand edit: same day if possible

Trim the pre-show and breaks, fix any live wobbles, correct captions, and publish within 24 hours while the event is still being talked about. Chapter it by session or topic so people can jump to what they need; an unchaptered three-hour recording is barely watched.

Highlights and social cuts

A 90-second highlight reel for the website and LinkedIn; four to eight 30–60-second vertical clips of the best moments (a strong quote, a demo, a laugh) for social; and a 10–15-minute "if you missed it" version of the keynote. Prioritise moments over messages: the clip that travels is the human one. Deliver them within 48 hours; a week later they're history.

Captions, transcripts and the written layer

Corrected captions on every video (accessibility and search). A full transcript on the event page. A written summary or blog post per session, which is what search engines actually index. Pull quotes for social. This layer costs little and is where most of the long-tail audience arrives.

Gated or open?

Gating the on-demand version behind registration captures leads but cuts viewing by a large factor. A common compromise: keynote and highlights open (reach), full sessions gated (leads). For internal events, open to staff via SSO, with a reminder campaign.

The 30-day distribution plan

WhenWhat
Day 0–1Thank-you email with on-demand link; highlight reel on social
Day 2–5Social clips daily; written session summaries published
Week 2Best session promoted to non-attendees; speaker shares their clip
Week 3Q&A follow-up: unanswered questions answered in a post or short video
Week 4Report on-demand numbers; decide what becomes evergreen content

Measure it

On-demand views and watch time, clip reach, transcript page visits, leads from gated content. Report them alongside the live numbers (see metrics) and the event's ROI story roughly doubles.
