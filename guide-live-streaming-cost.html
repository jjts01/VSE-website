What does live streaming actually cost?

Honest UK numbers and what drives them — so you can budget before you ask anyone for a quote.

3 min read · Updated September 2026

Streaming costs scale with four things: cameras, hours, destinations and risk. Here's how that plays out in the UK market in 2026. All figures exclude VAT and assume a single show day; use our budget estimator for a number tailored to your event.

Simple webinar or boardroom stream — roughly £750–£1,500

One camera, slides, a streaming engineer with an encoder, one destination (Teams, YouTube, LinkedIn). The cost is mostly the engineer's day and the kit. Add a remote presenter and it edges toward the top of the range.

Single-room conference or town hall — roughly £2,000–£5,000

Two to three cameras, vision mixing, a dedicated stream audio mix, graphics and lower thirds, redundant encoding, possibly a remote speaker or two, a crew of two to three. This is the most common brief we see, and the range reflects how much redundancy and graphics polish the client wants.

Hybrid conference or awards show — £5,000–£15,000+

Multi-camera, full graphics package, an event platform with registration and Q&A, remote contributors managed on broadcast links, a rehearsal day, redundant everything. The platform and the rehearsal day are the items people forget to budget, and the two that most affect whether the show goes well.

What moves the number up

  • Multiple sessions or rooms streamed simultaneously (each room is effectively its own production).
  • Pre-records and edit work before the event.
  • Custom platform builds or complex registration.
  • International remote speakers on managed links, with out-of-hours rehearsals.
  • Same-day highlight edits and social cuts.
  • Human live captioning and BSL interpretation.

What moves it down

  • A venue whose in-house AV can supply a clean audio split and camera feeds.
  • Flexible timings that let a crew do the tech run the same day.
  • Using a cloud gallery instead of physical production kit on site.
  • Remote operation — a remote vMix operator runs the show for a fraction of an on-site crew.
  • Standard graphics templates rather than bespoke motion packages.

Day rates you'll see on quotes

UK broadcast crew rates in 2026 typically sit in these bands: streaming or vision engineers £400–£550 per day; video HOD or technical director £550–£700; production manager £500–£650; editor £350–£500. Production machines (a vMix system, say) run £350–£500 per day; bonded cellular encoders £250–£550 per job. Travel, accommodation and consumables are billed at cost. Multi-day bookings usually attract a reduction.

The cheapest insurance you can buy

Redundancy — a second encoder and a bonded backup connection — typically adds a few hundred pounds. Set that against the cost of your event going dark in front of the whole company and it's the easiest line on the budget to approve.
